SLIDE 02 // OPERATIONAL CONSTRAINTS

THE SOUTH POLAR ILLUMINATION & COMMS PARADOX

Why Site Selection at the Lunar South Pole is an Astrodynamic Puzzle
1. GRAZING SOLAR GEOMETRY (θ ≤ 6.7°)

The Moon’s axial tilt is only 1.543° to the ecliptic. Combined with its 5.145° orbital inclination, the Sun never climbs higher than 6.7° above the polar horizon. Modest crater rims cast kilometers-long shadows, creating Permanently Shadowed Regions (PSRs, T < 40 K) holding billions of tons of water ice directly beside illuminated ridge peaks.

2. LUNAR LIBRATION & DTE DROPOUTS

Because the Moon is tidally locked, Earth hovers along the horizon. Optical and physical libration causes Earth to oscillate ±8.1° in longitude and ±6.9° in latitude. During negative libration cycles, Earth dips behind local massifs, cutting direct communication for days without orbital relays.
