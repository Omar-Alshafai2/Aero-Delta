🚨 THE CRITICAL PROBLEM // WHY MISSIONS FAIL
1. Extreme Grazing Sunlight (< 2° Elevation):
Sun skims the polar horizon, casting elongated shadows from crater rims into cryogenic Permanently Shadowed Regions (PSRs) as low as -248 °C (25 K).

Rigorous Mathematical Formulations Physics
1. TOPOCENTRIC SOLAR ELEVATION (h)
sin(h) = sin(φ)·sin(δ) + cos(φ)·cos(δ)·cos(H)
φ = Selenographic Latitude, δ = Solar Declination, H = Local Hour Angle
2. TOPOCENTRIC AZIMUTH (A)
A = atan2(sin(H), cos(H)·sin(φ) - tan(δ)·cos(φ)) + 180°
Quadrant-resolved azimuth measured clockwise from true north (0° to 360°)
3. ATMOSPHERE-FREE HORIZON MASKING
IsIlluminated = (hSun + RSun > θobs(ASun))
Includes solar disk angular semi-diameter (RSun ≈ 0.27°) for grazing penumbral rays.

Polar Energy Realities Engineering
Vertical Mast Arrays:

Because polar sun elevation is only 1.5°–2.0°, horizontal panels capture sin(2.0°) ≈ 3.5% flux. AERO-DELTA models azimuth-tracking vertical mast arrays capturing cos(1.5°–2.0°) ≈ 99.9% flux.

Thermal Blackouts (as low as -248 °C / 25 K):

When crater shadows pass over the lander, temperatures drop rapidly. AERO-DELTA displays exact countdowns to shadow entry so science drills stop in time to conserve battery heating.

Launch Window Agility:

A 3-day launch slip can transform a 180-hour continuous sunlight window into an immediate blackout. AERO-DELTA allows mission directors to simulate alternative dates instantly.

Why Libration Dictates Mission Budget Economics

Direct-to-Earth vs Lunar Relay:
Commercial CLPS landers can communicate directly with NASA's Deep Space Network (DSN) during positive elevation windows, minimizing dependence on commercial orbital relay satellites.

The Libration "Trap":
Because the Moon wobbles ±6.7° in latitude and ±7.9° in longitude, Earth appears to trace an elliptical box in the polar sky. A landing site with good visibility on Day 1 can lose line-of-sight on Day 14.

AERO-DELTA's Operational Edge:
The platform computes the exact Earth elevation angle across the entire planned mission timeline, warning teams if Earth dips behind an obstructive mountain ridge.
