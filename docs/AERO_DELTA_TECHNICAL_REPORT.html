ABSTRACT
Future lunar exploration under NASA's Artemis campaign, Commercial Lunar Payload Services (CLPS), and international partnerships—such as Canada's Lunar Rover initiative—concentrates heavily on the lunar south polar region. The complex selenographic geometry at high latitudes (≥ 80°S), combined with low solar elevation angles (≤ 6.7°) and severe Earth libration obscuration, imposes unprecedented constraints on solar power generation and direct-to-Earth (DTE) communication. This paper introduces Aero-Delta, an open-architecture computational platform engineered for rapid scenario evaluation by mission architects and systems engineers, combining a deterministic topocentric mechanics engine, 3D WebGL topography, sub-arcminute parity with NASA JPL Horizons (< 0.03° residual), and deep GIS integration with LROC QuickMap and NASA Moon Trek.

1. INTRODUCTION & MISSION OPERATIONAL CONTEXT

The Moon's rotational axis is tilted only 1.543° with respect to the ecliptic pole. Combined with the 5.145° inclination of the lunar orbit to the ecliptic, the Sun's maximum elevation above the local horizontal plane anywhere in the south polar region never exceeds 6.7°.

Because the Sun perpetually skims the polar horizon, elevated topographical features intercept near-permanent solar radiation ("peaks of eternal light"). Conversely, deep crater interiors never receive direct sunlight over billions of years, creating Permanently Shadowed Regions (PSRs) where temperatures plunge below 40 K (-233°C), functioning as cold traps for volatile water ice deposits.

2. CELESTIAL MECHANICS & TOPOCENTRIC MATHEMATICS

Calculations are anchored to the standard J2000.0 barycentric astronomical epoch (JD 2451545.0).

2.1 Solar Topocentric Coordinates

sin(θ_sun) = sin(φ_site) · sin(δ_sun) + cos(φ_site) · cos(δ_sun) · cos(H_sun)

Where θ_sun is the apparent solar elevation above the local airless horizon, φ_site is observer selenographic latitude, and H_sun is the local hour angle driven by the 29.530589-day synodic period.

2.2 Earth Libration Dynamics

L(t) = [ l(t), b(t) ] = [ ±8.1° · sin(2πd / 27.55455d), ±6.9° · sin(2πd / 27.2122d) ]

Optical and physical libration models optical displacement in longitude (anomalistic cycle of 27.55 days) and latitude (draconic nodal cycle of 27.21 days).
